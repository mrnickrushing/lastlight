# World bible

## Canon

The world is called **the Veilwild**. Long ago, settlements used First Lanterns
to turn remembered acts of care, courage, craft, and grief into protective light.
The system was created to preserve communities, but the buried central mechanism
began consuming unwanted memories. Those abandoned memories accumulated into
**the Long Night**, a weather-like intelligence that erases names and reshapes
living things.

Last Light begins at **Emberhollow**, the final town whose First Lantern still
burns. Its keeper disappeared after removing seven memory seals from the machine
below. The player arrives carrying the eighth seal without knowing why.

### Lore rules

- Light is memory made shareable; it is not generic holy magic.
- Darkness is not automatically evil. Forgotten grief becomes dangerous when
  denied, while chosen darkness can hide, rest, and protect.
- The monsters are transformed memories, animals, tools, weather, and people;
  they must reflect the region that created them.
- The First Lantern cannot solve problems alone. It amplifies relationships and
  choices already made.
- Nobody is “the chosen one.” The town survives through accumulated contributions.
- Death exists in the past and story, but named residents do not die permanently
  from routine systemic failure. Chapter decisions may include sacrifice only
  with clear narrative framing and no gameplay purchase.

## Emberhollow

Emberhollow begins as a damaged camp around a stone lantern tower and becomes a
layered town. Its silhouette must remain recognizable at every tier: central
lantern, rising ring walls, seven district lights, and the dark mountain opening
behind it.

### Town tiers

| Tier | Name | Visible transformation | Major unlock |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | Last Camp | tents, broken wall, failing central lantern | tutorial and Bramblewake |
| 1 | Kindled Hamlet | repaired homes and workbench row | crafting, resident jobs |
| 2 | Rooted Village | palisade ring and greenhouses | farming, companions |
| 3 | Lantern Borough | stone gate and market square | visiting, contracts |
| 4 | Watchtown | towers, lift, fortified lanes | advanced defense and coast |
| 5 | Beacon City | district lanterns and aqueduct | region logistics and research |
| 6 | Veilward | runic wall, observatory, rail line | final seals and mastery |
| 7 | Last Light | restored lantern network across skyline | finale and ending state |

### Districts

1. **Lantern Square** — First Lantern, archive, town board, celebrations.
2. **Builder's Row** — workbench, forge, mill, engineer yard, construction plots.
3. **Hearthmarket** — kitchen, inn, stalls, social commons, trade post.
4. **Greenward** — garden, apothecary, waterworks, companion habitats.
5. **Scholar's Rise** — library, observatory, runic laboratory, map room.
6. **Beast Yard** — stables, training grounds, rescue pens, trail gate.
7. **Watch Ring** — walls, towers, guardhouse, traps, emergency stores.
8. **Deep Gate** — locked mountain entrance, lift machinery, final expedition.

Each district has three functional restoration states and cosmetic customization
slots. Function is earned through play; Robux may buy visual themes only after a
district's gameplay state is unlocked.

## Launch story

### Prologue — The Eighth Seal

The player wakes outside Emberhollow with a sealed lantern and rescues **Mara
Venn**, the town carpenter. The first defense reveals that creatures are calling
the player by the missing keeper's name. The player chooses food, walls, or
medicine as the town's first priority; this changes scenes and convenience, not
permanent power.

### Chapter I — What the Roots Remember

**Region:** Bramblewake Woods  
**Question:** Can a place forgive the people who abandoned it?  
**Story:** The forest has grown through farms evacuated during the first Long
Night. Root-bound echoes repeat the evacuation. The party rescues farmers,
reconnects sap channels, and learns the missing keeper deliberately awakened the
Warden Stag to guard a memory seal.  
**Blackout rule:** walls root in place; players must carry lantern fire between
living root nodes.  
**Boss:** The Warden Stag. Breaking every antler harms the forest; cleansing its
heart preserves a future traversal ally.  
**Permanent decision:** restore farmland, allow wild regrowth, or create a shared
agroforest. All are viable and change Greenward appearance, recipes, and events.

### Chapter II — The Bell Beneath Iron

**Region:** Ironroot Delve  
**Question:** What does a town owe the people whose labor built it?  
**Story:** Old mining engines continue working without crews. The player finds
recordings showing Emberhollow's first walls were built with unsafe forced shifts.
Rescued miners demand the town archive tell the truth.  
**Blackout rule:** darkness travels through rail lines; teams reroute carts and
power while defending moving objectives.  
**Boss:** Bellows Maw, a furnace-engine that inhales loose objects and exhales
molten slag.  
**Permanent decision:** memorialize and close the deep mine, reopen it under
resident control, or convert it to geothermal works.

### Chapter III — Faces in Mireglass

**Region:** Mireglass Fen  
**Question:** Which version of yourself deserves to be remembered?  
**Story:** Mirror pools create helpful and hostile copies of residents based on
memories they tried to discard. The party follows conflicting copies of the
missing keeper and meets **Ilyra Quill**, a runic historian hiding her involvement
in the First Lantern project.  
**Blackout rule:** each player gains a mirrored double that repeats recent
actions; coordinated players use doubles to solve defenses.  
**Boss:** The Lantern Witch, a composite reflection that steals player silhouettes
and profession abilities with clear counterplay.  
**Permanent decision:** preserve the mirror archive, break it, or entrust it to
residents with consent-based access.

### Chapter IV — The Storm That Waited

**Region:** Tempest Reach  
**Question:** Is preparation still courage when the feared disaster arrives?  
**Story:** A cliff observatory predicted the Long Night but isolated itself rather
than warning nearby ships. The party repairs signal towers across a ship graveyard
while a repeating storm compresses time.  
**Blackout rule:** lightning charges defenses and enemies; players intentionally
route strikes through grounded networks.  
**Boss:** The Tidebound Titan, a wreck-and-coral giant fought across changing
water levels.  
**Permanent decision:** rebuild the harbor, preserve it as a rescue station, or
turn the observatory into an open warning network.

### Chapter V — The Warmth Under Frostmere

**Region:** Frostmere Vale  
**Question:** How much safety becomes isolation?  
**Story:** Monks preserved memories by freezing every strong emotion. Their buried
community is peaceful and unable to change. The party thaws selected archives and
helps residents experience grief without being consumed by it.  
**Blackout rule:** players share a moving circle of warmth; separating creates
useful paths but dangerous exposure.  
**Boss:** The White Howler, an aurora wolf that hunts noise, footprints, and
isolated targets.  
**Permanent decision:** gradual thaw, protected sanctuary, or mobile archive.

### Chapter VI — A City Made of Ash

**Region:** Cinderfall Crown  
**Question:** Can rebuilding honor what cannot be restored?  
**Story:** A foundry-city tried to manufacture artificial memory light. Its glass
streets replay idealized lives while ash creatures maintain an empty performance.
The missing keeper used its core to open the Deep Gate.  
**Blackout rule:** light creates solid glass paths but also reveals players to the
Ash Regent; darkness hides but dissolves paths.  
**Boss:** The Ash Regent, a ceremonial construct that rebuilds itself from false
statues until players expose authentic resident memories.  
**Permanent decision:** rebuild one living neighborhood, preserve the ruin, or
salvage the city to strengthen every surviving settlement.

### Chapter VII — The Hollow Below

**Region:** The Hollow Below  
**Question:** Does healing require remembering everything?  
**Story:** The party descends through the seven original lantern chambers. The
missing keeper, **Orin Vale**, is alive but partially merged with the Long Night.
He removed the seals because the machine was erasing painful memories from the
entire region. The Long Night is the accumulated remainder asking to be witnessed.
Each chapter decision changes who appears, which routes open, and which memories
can stabilize the finale.  
**Blackout rule:** the entire chapter is one controlled Blackout with rest
sanctuaries. Town residents operate systems remotely, reflecting jobs and bonds.
**Boss:** The Nameless Night, fought as a sequence of defense, pursuit, rescue,
and choice rather than a health sponge.

### Endings

All endings preserve continued play:

- **The Shared Flame:** integrate painful memory into a consent-based lantern
  network. Balanced chapter decisions and strong bonds make this easiest.
- **The Free Night:** shut down memory conversion and teach towns to survive with
  ordinary craft, community, and limited runes.
- **The New Keeper:** rebuild the machine with strict safeguards and a rotating
  resident council. Fastest recovery, highest future responsibility.

The epilogue updates Emberhollow, region weather, NPC schedules, and repeatable
contracts. It never resets the player's town.

## Regions

### Bramblewake Woods

- **Silhouette:** immense arching roots, windmills swallowed by trees, amber sap.
- **Traversal:** climbable roots, rope lines, hollow trunks, shallow streams.
- **Hazards:** grasping bramble, pollen sleep, falling limbs, moving hedges.
- **Resources:** heartwood, amber sap, meadow fiber, brightcap, spring clay.
- **Landmarks:** Crooked Farm, Root Cathedral, Foxfire Mill, Evacuation Road.
- **Town unlocks:** sawmill, garden, ropeworks, farm plots.
- **Palette:** moss, wet bark, wheat gold, lantern amber.

### Ironroot Delve

- **Silhouette:** vertical shafts, iron roots, red rail lights, fungal vaults.
- **Traversal:** mine carts, lifts, cranes, breakable supports, pressure doors.
- **Hazards:** cave-in, gas, slag, unstable rails, crushing machinery.
- **Resources:** ironroot ore, coalglass, glow fungus, machine oil, echo crystal.
- **Landmarks:** Shift Bell, Red Junction, Abandoned Canteen, Furnace Heart.
- **Town unlocks:** forge, rail delivery, lift, advanced traps.
- **Palette:** soot, oxidized iron, furnace orange, fungus teal.

### Mireglass Fen

- **Silhouette:** flooded roofs, reflective black pools, pale reeds, hanging lights.
- **Traversal:** punts, stepping roots, drained channels, reflection portals.
- **Hazards:** deep mud, false ground, memory doubles, toxic bloom.
- **Resources:** mirror reed, bog iron, witchlight pollen, eel oil, blackwater herb.
- **Landmarks:** Drowned Bell, Three-Faced House, Glass Chapel, Heron Causeway.
- **Town unlocks:** apothecary, waterworks, rune lenses, consent archive.
- **Palette:** ink blue, reed gray, spectral mint, muted violet.

### Tempest Reach

- **Silhouette:** sea cliffs, broken ships, rotating lighthouse beams, storm walls.
- **Traversal:** ziplines, climbing anchors, tide caves, sail lifts.
- **Hazards:** lightning, wind shear, high tide, collapsing rigging.
- **Resources:** storm copper, salt crystal, sailcloth, coral bone, charged glass.
- **Landmarks:** Last Harbor, Signal Teeth, Wreck Orchard, Eye Observatory.
- **Town unlocks:** observatory, weather warning, harbor trade, lightning rods.
- **Palette:** slate, sea green, lightning white, beacon red-orange.

### Frostmere Vale

- **Silhouette:** frozen lake, bell monastery, ice bridges, aurora caves.
- **Traversal:** sliding, ice anchors, thermal vents, sound-sensitive snow.
- **Hazards:** exposure, thin ice, avalanche, noise hunters, whiteout.
- **Resources:** frostglass, wool moss, blue salt, ember lichen, aurora thread.
- **Landmarks:** Silent Abbey, Underlake Road, Warm Crypt, Aurora Mouth.
- **Town unlocks:** insulated storehouse, bathhouse, cold storage, quiet wards.
- **Palette:** moon blue, snow gray, aurora green, hearth red.

### Cinderfall Crown

- **Silhouette:** black trees, glass boulevards, giant foundry crown, ash snow.
- **Traversal:** light-made bridges, cooling channels, elevators, brittle glass.
- **Hazards:** heat, ash blindness, glass fracture, false-memory loops.
- **Resources:** cindersteel, memory glass, ash silk, kiln pearl, crown coal.
- **Landmarks:** Parade of None, Glass Homes, Common Kiln, Regent Foundry.
- **Town unlocks:** master forge, glassworks, district lighting, final defenses.
- **Palette:** charcoal, ember coral, smoked glass, royal gold degraded by ash.

### The Hollow Below

- **Silhouette:** impossible lantern machinery descending into a starless void.
- **Traversal:** rotating chambers, memory bridges, resident-operated lifts,
  profession solutions, paths formed from chapter decisions.
- **Hazards:** name loss, reversed controls with clear warning, shifting gravity,
  memory storms, copied encounters.
- **Resources:** no normal farming; only seal fragments and final craft components.
- **Landmarks:** Seven Chambers, Keeper's Room, Discard Sea, First Furnace.
- **Town unlocks:** ending state and postgame network.
- **Palette:** bone, void indigo, every region's accent returning near the finale.

## Factions

- **Emberhollow Council:** residents form it gradually; it is not a static ruler.
- **The Wayfarers:** scouts and couriers keeping paths between surviving camps.
- **Iron Union:** miners and engineers demanding truthful archives and safe work.
- **Glass Readers:** historians who can interpret reflected memories.
- **Stormwatch:** rescuers and signal keepers along Tempest Reach.
- **The Quiet Order:** Frostmere preservers divided over thawing.
- **Ash Masque:** memory constructs performing a dead city's idealized routine.
- **Nightkin:** intelligent transformed creatures; some are allies or companions.

## Environmental storytelling rules

- Every landmark communicates who used it, what changed, and what can change next.
- Collectibles expand a visible location or person; no lore is only a detached log.
- Critical story facts appear through at least two modes: scene plus environment,
  dialogue plus archive, or mechanics plus recap.
- Repeated expeditions show changed aftermath rather than resetting every story set.
- Player construction never erases a resident's essential access route.
